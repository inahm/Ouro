Work and experiments

Nota

An iOS-native app for reviewing footage. Log moments, write notes, find what matters.

The problem is simple: video projects can create hundreds of hours of footage, and the important moments are easy to lose. A quote, a gesture, a pause, a reaction, a scene marker, a detail that matters later. Once it disappears into the timeline, finding it again can take forever.

Nota helps anyone working with video footage review with more intention. Users can log time-stamped moments, write notes, organize clips, and keep track of what matters as they watch.

We also built Nota to get closer to the native app-making process itself: learning Xcode, understanding iOS patterns, and working through what it takes to design, build, and prepare something for the App Store.

It’s both a useful tool and a hands-on exploration of how modern product teams can move from idea to native software with more ownership.

We expect to launch Nota in the App Store by summer 2026.

Overture DS

A design system workflow for shaping, querying, and evolving systems with agents.

We’re not just building an agentic design system. We’re building the workflow around it, so designers like us can still shape it visually when we need to, query it structurally, and extend it across products, tools, and teams through natural language prompts.

Today, JSON is the connective layer. It turns our design decisions into something structured enough to adapt across an evolving tooling landscape, precise enough to support implementation, and clear enough for agents to reason over.

This is where agents and code editors become integral. Not as a shortcut around design judgment, but as a way to help the system respond and adapt quickly to new contexts, platforms, and brand languages. Agents can read the structure, compose UI from prompts, check for consistency, adapt rules across contexts, and help generate new patterns that still belong.

We’re still early, but that’s the point. The work is not just the design system. It is the way the system gets made, used, and evolved.

Health Atlas

An early personal experiment in more connected care.

This project started as a very personal experiment, born from our own frustration: trying to hold a more holistic, longitudinal view of our health in a system that was never designed to hold the whole story.

Especially in women’s health, the details matter: random symptoms, labs, cycles, medications, history, lifestyle, and lived experience. But they end up scattered across portals, PDFs, notes, and memory. Too much of the story lives between appointments, and too often, we’re the ones piecing it back together.

Health Atlas turns the records we already have into a personal health archive we can actually use. AI helps organize the details, surface patterns, notice what has changed, and prepare better questions for the next appointment.

The question we’re trying to answer is simple, and one many women are already asking: what if a clear picture of our health didn’t have to live behind expensive concierge medicine, membership clinics, or fragmented hospital portals?
