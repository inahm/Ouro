  [Nota] — featured card
  An OS-native app for reviewing video footage, logging moments, and finding
  what matters.
  Button: Learn more / Close
  Expanded: #hcard-detail-nota
    (Body) The problem is simple: video projects can create hundreds of hours
    of footage, and the important moments are easy to lose. A quote, a
    gesture, a pause, a reaction, a scene marker, a detail that matters later.
    Once it disappears into the timeline, finding it again can take forever.
    Nota helps anyone working with video footage review with more intention.
    Users can log time-stamped moments, write notes, organize clips, and keep
    track of what matters as they watch.
    We also built Nota to get closer to the native app-making process itself:
    learning Xcode, understanding iOS patterns, and working through what it
    takes to design, build, and prepare something for the App Store.
    It’s both a useful tool and a hands-on exploration of how modern product
    teams can move from idea to native software with more ownership.
    We expect to launch Nota in the App Store by summer 2026.
    CTA (link): “Request early access” →
      mailto:hello@ourolabs.com?subject=Nota%3A%20Request%20early%20access&body=Hi%20Ouro%20team%2C%0D%0A%0D%0AI%27d%20like%20to%20request%20early%20access%20to%20Nota.%0D%0A%0D%0AThanks%2C%0D%0A

  [Overture DS]
  We're building a workflow for designers to shape, query, and evolve design
  systems alongside agents.
  Expanded: #hcard-detail-overture
    (Body) We’re not just building an agentic design system. We’re building the
    workflow around it, so designers like us can still shape it visually when
    we need to, query it structurally with agent support, and extend it across products, tools,
    and teams through natural language prompts.
    Today, JSON is the connective layer. It turns our design decisions into
    something structured enough to adapt across an evolving tooling
    landscape, precise enough to support implementation, and clear enough for
    agents to reason over.
    This is where agents and code editors become integral. Not as a shortcut
    around design judgment, but as a way to help the system respond and adapt
    quickly to new contexts, platforms, and brand languages. Agents can read
    the structure, compose UI from prompts, check for consistency, adapt
    rules across contexts, and help generate new patterns that still belong.
    We’re still early, but that’s the point. The work is not just the design
    system. It is the way the system gets made, used, and evolved.

  [Health Atlas]
  A personal experiment for understanding your health with more context and
  continuity.
  Expanded: #hcard-detail-health-atlas
    (Body) This project started as a very personal experiment, born from our
    own frustration trying to hold a more holistic, longitudinal view of our
    health in a system that was never designed to hold the whole story.
    Especially when it comes to women’s health, the details matter: random symptoms, labs,
    cycles, medications, history, lifestyle, and lived experience. But they end
    up scattered across portals, PDFs, notes, and memory. Too much of the
    story lives between appointments, and too often, we’re the ones piecing it
    back together.
    Health Atlas turns the records we already have into a personal health
    archive we can actually use. AI helps organize the details, surface
    patterns, notice what has changed, and prepare better questions for the
    next appointment.
    The question we’re trying to answer is simple, and one many people are
    also asking: what if a clear picture of our health didn’t have to live
    behind expensive concierge medicine, membership clinics, or fragmented
    hospital portals?
